Why this exists

Hard-to-find information, made public and plain

Arkansas is looking at one of the largest buildouts of computing power in its history. The facts are scattered across trackers, utility filings, county minutes, company pages, and local news, and some of it sits behind paywalls and NDAs.

Grey Swan gathers what is public into one place, marks clearly what is still unknown, and walks through each project one location at a time. Clarksville is first. As we finish each facility page, we release it here, and we aim to put breaking news on this hub first, then on Substack and socials.

The goal is simple: help neighbors, officials, and builders see the same facts, so each community can be a partner in the deal and not just a host.

This model works for any state. We are starting with Arkansas because this is home. The same public-records method (gather, verify, mark the unknowns, report in plain English) can travel.

A note on language. News reports and some project names use an older term. We call these compute campuses, because that is what they could be: community partnerships and real educational facilities where everyone can win.

Why this is a grey swan

What Grey Swan research looks like

A grey swan, as we use the term, is 80% likely to 100% inevitable. These events happen all the time. Some are known and planned around, though people could be better prepared. Compute campuses are one: AI is here to stay, power is tight, and the buildout is already underway in Arkansas.

Steve's working forecast (judgment, not a promise)

Every new technology meets a rush, then fear, then argument. Over the next 12 months, we will learn how much this technology can help the people who need it most. As that becomes clearer, a lot of the fear will ease, and this can become a bipartisan solution, especially with nuclear power and nuclear fusion. That is not a promise that a fusion plant arrives next year, or that either party has already signed on. It is why communities should negotiate now, while uncertainty still leaves room for a better deal.

Explainer

Who is Aterio?

Aterio is a commercial research firm that tracks compute facilities across the U.S. Its public Arkansas summary is where the 34-facility and 3,618 MW figures come from.

Aterio counts individual buildings or phases as facilities, so 34 facilities is not 34 separately named campuses.

  • It tracks facilities. It counts buildings, estimated megawatts, and status (operating, under construction, announced).
  • It does not own them. Aterio is not a developer, an owner, or a party to any Arkansas deal.
  • Its full list is paid. The facility-level dataset is sold. Its public summary names providers and totals, not every building.
  • Estimates, not committed load. Aterio's MW figures are estimates. Developer figures sometimes differ, and we show both.
  • Paid is not the same as secret. A paywall on commercial research does not by itself prove anyone hid a government deal.

Our argument, clearly labeled

Secrecy keeps neighbors out of the room

There are two documented reasons names are missing: Aterio sells its full list, and real negotiations often run through NDAs, project LLCs, and unnamed end users. In Grant County, a second proposal near Redfield came to light in October despite signed NDAs, with the developer's name redacted.

Steve's civic argument is that the secrecy itself is the problem. When a community can't see who is coming, what was promised, or who will own the power, it can't judge whether it is a partner. That is an argument about how deals are made. It is not an allegation of a crime or misconduct by anyone.

For reporters and news outlets

The reference on Arkansas compute campuses and technology

Grey Swan keeps a sourced, regularly updated record of every named Arkansas compute-campus project we can document: status, megawatts, power, deal terms, owners and tenants where public, and what is still not public. Journalists are welcome to use it for background and cite it. Link the facility page and the date updated. If a figure looks off, tell us. We correct from public records, not from rumor.

We do not invent unnamed facilities. We do not allege misconduct. When a name is missing, we say so and point to the public reason we can document (a commercial paywall, an NDA, or a project LLC with no parent named).
