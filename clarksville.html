Who holds what

Where the power sits in this deal

Developer side

Serverfarm / Solaris

Owns and runs the ~660 MW on-site generation and the campus. Keeps the economics of the power.

Public side

City of Clarksville & Johnson County

Holds title for bond financing (not the same as owning the power). Receives PILOT, grants, fees, and lease rent.

Local utility

Clarksville Connected Utilities

18 MW for construction only. The turbines stay off the local lines, which protects residents from the immediate load.

What we know

  • Four buildings, ARK1–ARK4, at 108 MW each: 432 MW IT.
  • About 40 Solaris gas turbines, about 660 MW, on the developer's side of the meter.
  • The city is not liable for bond repayment.
  • 65% abatement for up to 30 years; $548M PILOT projected through 2060.
  • $1M each to the city, the county, and Johnson Regional Medical Center.
  • 43 city acres leased to Solaris at $1,000 a month for five years, for staging and laydown.
  • Hatchbo LLC is described as the tenant of the first two buildings.
  • Serverfarm publishes planned workforce partnerships with local schools, Arkansas Tech, and University of the Ozarks.

What we don't know yet

  • Which company is Hatchbo LLC's parent.
  • Who will use buildings 3 and 4.
  • Full turbine ownership and operating contracts.
  • Whether the full agreements include job guarantees, clawbacks, or decommissioning terms (none found in the material reviewed).
  • Which permit-fee figure is final ($840,000 or $2.1 million).
  • The timing and source of the later renewable supply.
  • Whether the lease land holds any part of the generation. The lease is not proof that it does.
Our take

Real benefits. The power stayed private.

Clarksville got real benefits and a real protection: the city is off the hook for the bonds, a hospital gets money, and residents are shielded from the immediate load. That is not a scandal. It is a deal worth reading.

Set beside those benefits are a long abatement, fixed land rent, and private ownership of a productive power asset. The piece we would keep public or shared is the generation, with a written share of the benefit going to care for vulnerable people, including Conway HDC.

That is a proposed alternative, not a term in Clarksville's deal and not a guarantee. What Clarksville can still do: ask for the full agreements; ask how rate protection, water, jobs, and decommissioning are reported; and explore fair terms at any genuinely open renewal.

This is civic analysis from public sources, not an allegation of wrongdoing by any named person or company.
